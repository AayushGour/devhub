# The Salt Roads

The caravans moved at night, when the sand was cold and the stars were a map. Dr. Ibn Battuta counted forty camels at the first well. Each carried salt cut from the pans at Taghaza, where the houses themselves were built of salt blocks.

Nobody lived at Taghaza by choice. The miners were sent, and they stayed, and the wind polished the walls of their salt houses until they shone.

# Crossing the Empty Quarter

Twenty days without a well. The guides navigated by the shape of the dunes and by a memory of stars that had been handed down for six generations.

A caravan that lost its guide lost everything. This happened often enough that the route was marked, in places, by the bones of those who had tried it alone.

# The Market at Timbuktu

Salt arrived as slabs and left as coins. A block the length of a man's forearm bought a measure of gold dust, and the scribes of Sankore recorded every exchange in a ledger that outlived the traders themselves.

The city grew rich on a mineral that most of the world scraped from the sea for nothing.
